Halifax & King George Wills name index

What this is

Search everyone named in the Halifax County, North Carolina and King George County, Virginia will abstracts on freeafricanamericans.com: the person making each will, every person given or lent something, and the enslaved people the wills name. Each result shows its will book, page and date, the original wording of the entry, and a link back to the entry in the source.

Please check the original before relying on any name

This is a finding aid, not a transcription of the wills. It indexes abstracts (short summaries) that another researcher made of the will books, and the names were then picked out of those abstracts, partly by a computer program. Names, relationships and gifts can be missing, misread or attached to the wrong person. Every record shows the abstract's exact wording and a link to the source page, so you can confirm what it says. For anything important, go on to the will book itself.

  • Not yet checkedThe names were picked out by a program and nobody has checked them yet. Expect some names or gifts to be missing; read the original text shown with the record.

Where the information comes from

All entries come from the will abstracts published at freeafricanamericans.com/halifax.htm, which cover:

  • Halifax County, North Carolina, Will Books Vol. 1 to Vol. 4 (1758 to 1854)
  • King George County, Virginia, Will Books A-1 and 1 to 4 (1721 to 1901)

The citations (will book, page, and the dates the will was written and proved) are the abstract's own. This index is not connected with that site or its author.

How the information was processed

  1. A saved copy of the source page was split into one entry per will, and each entry's exact wording was kept. You can read it under “Original text of this entry” on any record.
  2. Each entry was broken into three kinds of people: the will maker, each recipient (anyone given or lent something, with the relationship as the will states it), and each enslaved person the will names. Each gift was recorded as “gives” or “lends”, with what was given and any condition.
  3. The first 200 Halifax wills (Vol. 1 to Vol. 4, page 47) were read and broken down by hand. The remaining wills were first read by a program, which in testing missed about one recipient in six and about one enslaved person in three. Those wills are being checked by hand against the original text, and the label on each record tells you whether that has happened yet.
  4. Where a will names a child or wife by first name only (“my son Benjamin”), the will maker's surname was added so the person can be found by family name. Those names are marked “surname added”.
  5. Spellings are kept as the abstract gives them, including its question marks for unclear readings. Notes about doubtful readings appear on the record.

How to use this index

  • Search names: type any part of a name. Narrow by role (will maker, recipient, enslaved person) or by county and will book. “Include spelling variants” also finds names that sound alike, so Mathis finds Matthis and Wallace finds Wallis.
  • A–Z index: browse every will maker, recipient or enslaved person alphabetically by letter. Will makers and recipients are filed by surname; enslaved people, who are usually named by first name only, are filed by that name. Click a name to see its records, or a citation to go straight to that will.
  • Every name in a record is clickable and searches for that person, so you can follow a family or an enslaved person from will to will. Remember that the same name in different wills is not necessarily the same person.
  • A will maker's record lists each gift: who received it, whether it was given or lent, and what it was. A recipient's record shows what that person received.
  • “View this will in the source” opens the source page at that entry. “Copy these results as CSV” copies your current search for a spreadsheet.
